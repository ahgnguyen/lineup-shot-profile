# Lineup Shot Profile

**Live App** — https://lineup-shot-profile-lilac.vercel.app/

This tool attempts to predict where an NBA lineup is likely to shoot from on the court and its efficiency in those locations.

---

## What does it do?

Users can select any 5 players from across the NBA in the 2025-2026 season to create a lineup. By aggregating individual shot data from the season, the tool renders the shot profile you'd expect that group to produce. This predicted profile is visualized through a hexbin heatmap of the court where hex size indicates shot frequency and hex color indicates points per shot. The shot diet breakdown shown can also be viewed through different zones on the court. For lineups that have 100+ field goals attempted (FGA) together over the course of the season, the actual shot profile from the season can also be viewed and compared to the predicted profile.

In order to construct the profile for a lineup, data is pulled from every player's shot attempt across the 2025-2026 season. By using their FGA share in minutes that they are on the court, the predicted percentage of shots in the lineup that come from a certain player is calculated. The shot profile for a lineup is generated by combining this and the shooting efficiency of each player around the court.


---

## Why is it useful?

- Predict where the volume of shots for an opposing lineup are coming from and how efficient they are in various areas around the court. Highlight which areas should be given more attention, and gameplan defensive coverage against shots that the offense could be looking for.
- Evaluate how a certain lineup might fit on the court offensively. Inform on how the shot diet of a player would match with spacing and shot distribution in mind.
- Works for combinations of players that have never played together, or have limited time on the court together where the sample size of actual shot data isn't sufficient to make decisions.

## Technology Decisions

### Data (`nba_api`)

Uses the `nba_api` client, as opposed to other options, to fetch historical shot data as it is the maintained client for [stats.nba.com](http://stats.nba.com) and has the precise shot coordinate and lineup data that is necessary for this tool. Most other public APIs are only box-score level. Data is preloaded offline using Python scripts into Postgres rather than calling the API live so service downtime and rate limiting is not an issue.


### Database (Neon)

Using Postgres due to the scale of data (219,160 individual shot rows) and the fact that the relationship between players, their team, and their shots is naturally relational. This also makes querying for data efficient as the tool needs to be able to fetch data for different lineup selections quickly.

### Backend (Express)

Aggregating the selected lineup's shot history and performing computations is required to calculate and generate the shot profile. This is done server-side instead of on the client as it is more efficient as it reduces the data transfer necessary.


### Frontend (React / Vite / `d3-hexbin`)

The frontend is constructed using React as the interface is heavily stateful and interdependent. There are many different components between the court, the lineup selector, and the possible user selections that need to stay consistent. `d3-hexbin` is chosen for the profile visual to display the different bins as it is a common standard for NBA shot charts.

## Limitations and Improvements

Because this is a data driven tool, it's worth mentioning its limitations and possible improvements. These aspects should be considered before making decisions.

**Shot clock** 

Early-clock attempts better indicate what a lineup is looking for offensively. This is when an offense is running actions to generate open shots. For a defensive gameplan, the frequency and efficiency of these particular shots around the court better inform defensive coverage than late-clock attempts that a lot of the time are bailout shots. Being able to toggle between early and late shot clock data will help reduce noise in data.

**Shots that end in free throws** 

Shots that result in shooting fouls are not included in the dataset, so players who draw contact and get to the free throw line regularly are contributing less to the shot profile than they actually do in real life.

**Defensive scheme** 

Shot distribution is heavily determined by the defensive scheme being run and is not accounted for. Additionally, a potential next step would be inverting the tool and predicting what kind of looks a lineup gives up on the defensive end. This would allow a team to create an offensive gameplan to exploit the holes in a particular lineup.

**Lineup context** 

A player's role on the court heavily depends on who else is in the lineup. For example, their shot diet can drastically shift based on if they are sharing the floor with a high-usage ball handler.

**Game state** 

Garbage time is looser and higher pace, producing open shots that don't reflect the intensity of a normal game. Score margin and time remaining are valuable filters on the data.

**Second-chance points** 

There is no distinction between a first look and a second chance opportunity. How often a lineup generates offensive rebounds, and where those convert, is useful data for a lineup profile.

**Sample size** 

This tool only uses data from one season. Additional data for past seasons would improve accuracy as for most players, efficiency doesn't swing wildly from season to season.
